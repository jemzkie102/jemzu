Boracay

Western Visayas, Philippines · 10.32 km² · 37,802 (2020)
HOME | HISTORY OF BORACAY | GALLERY | ABOUT |

Geography & Demographics

  • 0.8 km off northwest Panay Island
  • 10.32 sq km (3.98 sq mi)
  • 3 barangays in Malay, Aklan
  • Population: 37,802 (2020)

History & People

  • Originally inhabited by Panay Bukidnon and Ati people
  • Commercial development → severe marginalization since the 1970s

Tourism & Recognition

  • White sand beaches, relaxation & nightlife
  • Travel + Leisure — Best Island in the World (2012)
  • Condé Nast Traveler — Best Islands in the World (2014)
  • Top 10 destinations to watch (2016)

2018 Closure & Reopening

  • April 2018: 6‑month closure (sewage renovation)
  • Managed by Boracay Inter‑agency Task Force
  • Reopened October 2018 with new rules
  • 30‑metre buffer zone · beachfront cleared

Boracay Ati-atihan

  • January 2024 — record 36,741 participants
  • Celebrates Ati indigenous heritage
  • Set to become a major tourism attraction starting 2025

Etymology

  • Inati: bora (bubbles) + bocay (white)
  • Local borac = white cotton
  • Spanish era: sagay (shell) + boray (seed)
  • "Bora" discouraged — ordinance (2011)
Note on the name "Bora" — Local stakeholders and the municipal government of Malay discourage the shortened "Bora" (derogatory, and to distinguish from Bora Bora). A February 2011 ordinance mandates the licensing office to refuse business licenses using "Bora" instead of "Boracay", and it is prohibited in promotional materials.
