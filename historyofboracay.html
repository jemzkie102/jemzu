Deep roots & shifting tides

Before the resorts and the worldwide fame, Boracay was home to the Ati people — and its name carries their legacy.

🪶

Indigenous Ati & the name Boracay

The island was originally populated by the Ati people. According to their oral tradition, the name Boracay comes from the Inati words "bora" (bubbles) and "bocay" (white).

bora + bocay = white bubbles
⛵

Spanish colonial period

Spanish colonizers recorded the island as Buracay. Early Spanish contact noted a small population of around 100 people who cultivated rice and raised goats.

🗺️

Administrative shifts

Boracay was historically part of the province of Capiz under the jurisdiction of Buruanga. It later became part of the municipality of Malay when it was established in 1949, and subsequently joined the independent province of Aklan in 1956.

🏝️

From farming & fishing to world stage

What was once a quiet farming and fishing community inhabited by the Ati people transformed into a world-famous beach destination. Today, Boracay balances its ancient heritage with global tourism.
